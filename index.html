⚙️ THE FOUR LAWS OF THERMODYNAMICS — APPLIED TO ABSORPTION REFRIGERATION

Law Name Statement Application to this Unit
0 Zeroth Law
Thermal Equilibrium		 If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
ΦA = ΦB ⟹ ΦA = ΦC
 The candle (ITEM 2), the check valve (ITEM 8), and the copper tubing all reach a common thermal state via radiation and conduction. This defines the temperature reference for the cycle.
1 First Law
Conservation of Energy		 Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.
ΔU = Q − W
 The candle supplies heat Qin. Part converts to work W (pressure rise), the rest raises internal energy ΔU. No energy is lost — it is redistributed.
2 Second Law
Heat Transfer Direction		 Heat flows spontaneously from a hotter body to a colder body, never the reverse.
∮ (δQ/T) ≤ 0 → ΔS ≥ 0
 Heat flows from flame → generator → capillary → evaporator, absorbing heat from the can. The cold can cannot re-heat the flame — this defines the cycle direction.
3 Third Law
Absolute Zero · Work as Energy		 As temperature approaches absolute zero (0 K), entropy approaches a minimum. Work is a form of energy transfer.
limT→0 S(T) = 0  ·  W = ∫ F·dx
 Theoretical lower bound for evaporator temperature is 0 K (−273.15 °C). In practice, the Pepsi can approaches ambient limits. Pressure work by vapor is a direct form of energy transfer.
PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION:
• The candle (ITEM 2) acts as heat reservoir — QH.
• The evaporator (ITEM 9) acts as cold reservoir — QC.
• Coefficient of Performance (COP):
    COPideal = TC / (TH − TC)
• Any real cycle has COPactual < COPideal due to friction and entropy increase.
